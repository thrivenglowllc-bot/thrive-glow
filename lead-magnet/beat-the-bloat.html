Beat the Bloat

A 7-day gut reset for women 30+
By Theresa, Integrative Health Practitioner (IHP 2)

If you're bloated by mid-afternoon, you're not imagining it, and it isn't "just your age." This week you'll make one small change a day and track how your body responds, so you can start to see your own patterns.

PART 1Why you're bloated

Most everyday bloating comes from a handful of common causes, and many women have more than one.

  1. 1
    Eating fast or on the go. Gulping meals means swallowing air and not chewing enough, so your gut has to work harder.
  2. 2
    Stress. Your gut and nervous system are closely linked. When you're rushed or anxious, digestion slows down.
  3. 3
    Low or sudden fiber. Too little fiber can slow things down; adding a lot at once can cause gas while your gut adjusts.
  4. 4
    Trigger foods and drinks. Fizzy drinks, sugar alcohols (like sorbitol in "sugar-free" products) and large portions of certain foods affect some women more than others.
  5. 5
    Hormone shifts. Bloating often changes across your cycle, and many women notice more of it in perimenopause.

This week is about noticing which of these show up for you.

PART 2The 7-day reset

Add one new habit each day and keep the ones before it. By day 7 you'll be doing all seven. Tick each box as you go.

DAY
1
Start your journal
Note what you eat, when, and how bloated you feel (1–10) morning, afternoon and evening. Change nothing else yet.
DAY
2
Slow down at meals
Sit down, put your fork down between bites, and chew each bite until it's soft. Aim for 20 minutes per meal.
DAY
3
Breathe before you eat
Take 5 slow breaths before each meal. It helps your body switch into "rest and digest" mode.
DAY
4
Hydrate between meals
Drink water through the day, mostly between meals rather than large amounts with them.
DAY
5
Skip the fizz and sugar alcohols
Swap sparkling drinks for still water or herbal tea, and check labels for sorbitol, xylitol and erythritol.
DAY
6
Take a 10-minute walk
A short, easy walk after your largest meal helps move things along.
DAY
7
Try a calming tea and an earlier dinner
Sip peppermint, ginger or fennel tea after dinner, and finish eating 2–3 hours before bed.

If you take medication or have a health condition, check with your doctor or pharmacist before adding herbal teas.

PART 4When to see your doctor

This guide is for everyday bloating. See your doctor promptly if you have any of these:

  • Blood in your stool, or black, tarry stools
  • Weight loss you can't explain
  • Severe or ongoing belly pain, or bloating that keeps getting worse
  • Vomiting, fever, or trouble swallowing
  • A lasting change in your bowel habits, especially if you're over 45
  • Symptoms that wake you up at night

What's next

If this week helped, keep the habits that made the biggest difference. If you're still bloated, that's useful information too: it often means something deeper, like gut imbalance, hormone shifts or food sensitivities, is worth looking into.
