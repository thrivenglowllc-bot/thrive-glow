Thyroid Health 101: What Every Woman Needs to Know

November 21, 2025 · {{owner_name}}

If you’ve been feeling “off” — tired no matter how much you sleep, struggling with stubborn weight, dealing with mood swings, hair shedding, or feeling like your hormones are running the show — your thyroid might be whispering (or screaming) for attention.

The thyroid is one of the most overlooked glands in women’s health… yet it’s one of the most powerful. When it’s out of alignment, everything feels out of alignment.

Let’s break it down in a friendly, science-backed way so you can understand what’s happening in your body — and what you can do to support it, naturally and holistically.

What the Thyroid Actually Does

Your thyroid is a small, butterfly-shaped gland located at the base of your neck… but don’t let its size fool you.

It is responsible for:

  • Metabolism (how your body burns energy)
  • Hormone production & regulation
  • Body temperature
  • Heart rate
  • Digestion speed
  • Brain clarity & mood
  • Skin, hair, and nail health
  • Menstrual cycle & fertility

Think of it as the master thermostat for your entire body.

When it’s balanced, everything runs smoothly.
When it’s imbalanced, you feel it everywhere.

Understanding T4, Free T3 & Reverse T3

Most women only get their TSH tested — and TSH doesn’t tell the full story. If you want to understand your thyroid, you need to know its three key hormones:

1. T4 — The Storage Hormone

T4 (thyroxine) is the thyroid hormone your body makes the most of.
But here’s the important part:

➡T4 isn’t active.
➡ It’s like money sitting in a savings account. You can’t spend it.

Your body must convert T4 into T3 for it to be effective.

2. Free T3 — The Active Hormone (Your Body’s “Gas Pedal”)

Free T3 is the active thyroid hormone that enters your cells and regulates metabolism, energy, mood, digestion, hair growth — everything.

If your Free T3 is low, even if TSH looks “normal,” you may experience:

  • Fatigue
  • Brain fog
  • Depression or anxiety
  • Cold hands/feet
  • Slow digestion
  • Weight gain or inability to lose weight
  • Slow periods or hormone irregularities

Low Free T3 = your body running on low battery mode.

3. Reverse T3 — The “Brake” Hormone

Reverse T3 (rT3) is your body’s protective mechanism.

When you’re stressed, inflamed, under-eating, over-exercising, or dealing with chronic illness, your body may convert T4 into reverse T3 instead of Free T3.

Reverse T3 BLOCKS active T3, slowing down metabolism and energy.

If Free T3 is the gas pedal,
Reverse T3 is the brake.

Symptoms Your Thyroid Is Out of Whack

Women over 30 are especially vulnerable to thyroid shifts due to:

  • Hormonal changes
  • Pregnancy/postpartum
  • Perimenopause
  • Chronic stress
  • Nutrient depletion
  • Autoimmunity

Some common signs include:

Metabolic Symptoms

  • Weight gain or stalled weight loss
  • Fatigue
  • Feeling cold all the time
  • Sluggish digestion or constipation

Hormonal Symptoms

  • PMS or irregular cycles
  • Heavy or light periods
  • Fertility challenges
  • Worsened perimenopause symptoms

Mood & Cognitive Symptoms

  • Anxiety or depression
  • Irritability
  • Brain fog
  • Low motivation

Physical Signs

  • Hair loss or thinning
  • Dry skin
  • Brittle nails
  • Swelling or puffiness (especially face + eyes)

If you’re experiencing 3–5 of these consistently, your thyroid may need deeper support.

How Thyroid Imbalances Affect Your Hormones

Your thyroid and your reproductive hormones are best friends — when one is off, the other struggles.

  • Low thyroid slows progesterone production → PMS, anxiety, irregular cycles
  • Low thyroid can disrupt ovulation → fertility issues
  • Thyroid dysfunction increases cortisol → worsened stress + inflammation
  • Imbalances can worsen perimenopause transitions
  • Low thyroid changes insulin sensitivity → weight struggles + cravings

Hormonal balance simply isn’t possible without thyroid balance.

How to Support Your Thyroid Naturally

1. Eat a Thyroid-Loving Diet

Include foods rich in:

  • Selenium: Brazil nuts, sardines, eggs (if tolerated)
  • Zinc: Oysters, beef, pumpkin seeds
  • Iodine: Seaweed, iodized salt, seafood
  • B vitamins: Grass-fed meats, nutritional yeast
  • Healthy fats: Olive oil, avocado, salmon
  • Antioxidants: Berries, leafy greens

Avoid:

  • Highly processed foods
  • Excess gluten (especially for Hashimoto’s)
  • Industrial seed oils
  • Excess sugar

2. Reduce Stress & Support Your Nervous System

Chronic stress → high cortisol → high reverse T3.

Support yourself with:

  • Slow morning routines
  • Breathwork
  • Gentle movement
  • More rest
  • Walking daily
  • Prayer + meditation

Sometimes the most healing thing you can do is slow down and let God speak into your life. Your body listens when your spirit is calm.

3. Support Your Gut

Your gut converts 20% of your T4 into T3.

Support gut health with:

  • Fiber-rich foods
  • Fermented foods (if tolerated)
  • Magnesium
  • Digestive bitters
  • Hydration
  • Pairing meals for steady blood sugar

4. Consider Supportive Supplements

Always check with your practitioner, but commonly helpful thyroid-supportive supplements include:

  • Selenium
  • Iodine (only if deficiency is confirmed)
  • Zinc
  • Vitamin D + K2
  • Omega 3s
  • Magnesium glycinate
  • Ashwagandha (for stress + thyroid conversion)
  • B-complex
  • Probiotics

5. Prioritize Rest

Regeneration happens when you sleep.
Hormones reset when you rest.
Your thyroid heals when you stop pushing through exhaustion.

Rest is productive — and holy.
