What I'm bringing into the New Year: Health, Wholeness, & Faith!

January 1, 2026 · {{owner_name}}

As the calendar turns and a new year begins, there’s always a quiet invitation to pause—not just to plan, but to reflect. In the past, I approached the new year with pressure: big goals, strict routines, and an unspoken belief that I needed to fix myself.

This year feels different.

Instead of chasing a “new me,” I’m choosing to carry forward what has truly supported my health, mental well-being, and faith—and to leave behind what no longer serves me. This new year isn’t about striving harder. It’s about living more intentionally, more aligned, and more rooted in what actually brings peace.

Here’s what I’m bringing into the new year—and why.

1. Health That Supports My Hormones, Not My Hustle

For a long time, I thought being healthy meant doing more—more workouts, more restriction, more discipline. But over the years (and especially as I’ve worked more deeply with women’s health and hormones), I’ve learned that the body thrives on support, not stress.

In this new year, I’m bringing:

  • Eating in a way that stabilizes blood sugar instead of skipping meals
  • Choosing protein-rich, nourishing foods that leave me energized—not depleted
  • Honoring where I am in my cycle instead of pushing through fatigue

A specific shift I’m keeping? Consistency over intensity. Even short walks, simple meals, and basic routines add up when done regularly. Health doesn’t need to feel punishing to be effective.

This year, my question isn’t:

“How hard can I push my body?”
It’s:
“How can I care for my body so it feels safe, supported, and strong?”

2. Wellness That Feels Calm, Not Complicated

Wellness used to feel overwhelming—so many supplements, routines, and opinions. I found myself constantly adding, rarely simplifying.

What I’m bringing into the new year is calm wellness.

That looks like:

  • Fewer products, but higher quality and more intentional choices
  • Morning routines that include quiet time instead of rushing
  • Nervous system regulation through breathwork, prayer, and rest

One small but powerful habit I’m continuing is protecting my mornings. Even 10–15 minutes without my phone—spent journaling, stretching, or praying—sets the tone for the entire day.

True wellness isn’t about doing everything “right.” It’s about creating space for your body and mind to breathe.

3. Mental Health Through Boundaries and Self-Compassion

Mental health is something I no longer treat as optional or secondary. This year, I’m bringing stronger boundaries and more grace.

That means:

  • Saying no without overexplaining
  • Stepping back from comparison on social media
  • Allowing myself to rest without earning it

One of the biggest lessons I’m carrying forward is this: growth doesn’t require constant self-criticism. You can want better for yourself and still be gentle with where you are.

I’m also bringing in regular check-ins with myself—asking:

“What do I need right now?”
Not what I should be doing, but what I truly need.

4. Faith as the Anchor for Everything Else

More than anything, I’m bringing my faith into the center of my life—not as an afterthought, but as the foundation.

This year, that looks like:

  • Journaling prayers instead of keeping everything in my head
  • Spending time in Scripture, even when life feels busy
  • Choosing trust over control when plans feel uncertain

There have been seasons where I tried to carry everything on my own—my health, my work, my future. What I’ve learned is that peace comes when I surrender the outcome and focus on obedience, presence, and trust.

When faith leads, everything else falls into place more naturally.

5. Intentional Living Instead of Constant Productivity

I’m no longer measuring success by how much I get done. I’m measuring it by how aligned and peaceful my life feels.

What I’m bringing into the new year:

  • Slower evenings
  • Fewer commitments that drain my energy
  • More space for relationships, reflection, and rest

Intentional living means choosing what matters most—and letting go of the rest without guilt.

As this new year begins, I’m not chasing a “new version” of myself. I’m choosing to be more grounded, more faithful, and more intentional with the life I already have.

I encourage you to pause, reflect, and ask:

“What do I want to carry forward—and what am I ready to release?”
You don’t need a massive plan. Try this instead:
  • Choose one habit that supports your health
  • Choose one practice that supports your mental well-being
  • Choose one rhythm that strengthens your faith

Write them down. pray, meditate, or visualize over them.
