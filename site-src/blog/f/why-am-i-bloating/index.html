Why am I bloating?

September 9, 2025 · {{owner_name}}

If you feel like no matter what you eat, you end up bloated, you’re not alone. So many women, including myself, once upon a time shared this same frustration—ordering a salad, sipping on a smoothie, or even eating something “healthy,” only to feel puffy, heavy, and uncomfortable afterward. Some even get to the point where eating becomes stressful because it feels like every bite will trigger bloating.

Here’s the truth: bloating isn’t something you just have to live with, and it’s not all in your head. It’s a signal from your body that something deeper is going on. By understanding why bloating happens, you can begin to take steps toward relief and, most importantly, healing the root cause.

Understanding Bloating:

Bloating often feels like your stomach is expanding like a balloon, but what’s happening inside your body is more complex. Here are some of the most common root causes:

  • Gas production from gut bacteria
    Your gut is home to trillions of bacteria, and when these bacteria break down certain foods, they release gas. While some gas is normal, an imbalance in your gut microbiome (too much of the “wrong” bacteria or yeast) can make this process excessive, leading to that uncomfortable bloated feeling.
  • Sluggish digestion
    If your stomach acid or digestive enzymes are low, food doesn’t break down properly. This can cause food to sit too long in your gut, where it ferments and creates gas. Stress can also play a role—when you’re in “fight-or-flight” mode, digestion slows down.
  • Hormonal influence
    Women often notice bloating gets worse at certain times of the month. That’s because progesterone, a hormone that naturally rises after ovulation, slows down digestion. Estrogen shifts can also affect water retention and gut motility, adding to the bloated feeling.
  • Gut imbalances
    An imbalance of gut bacteria, candida overgrowth, or low microbial diversity can all make your digestion more sensitive and reactive.
  • Food sensitivities
    Hidden sensitivities—often to foods like gluten, dairy, soy, or corn—can cause inflammation and trigger bloating. What makes this tricky is that reactions aren’t always immediate; they can show up hours later, making it hard to pinpoint the exact culprit.

Common Triggers That Make You Feel Bloated

Even when you’re eating “healthy,” certain habits and food choices can quietly be fueling your bloating. Here are some of the most common problems I see with women who feel like everything makes them bloat:

  • Eating too quickly – Swallowing food without chewing thoroughly leads to extra air in the gut and makes digestion harder.
  • High-FODMAP foods – Foods like onions, garlic, beans, and apples can be difficult to digest and feed gas-producing bacteria.
  • Carbonated drinks and artificial sweeteners – Sparkling water, soda, and sugar-free products can cause excess gas or irritation.
  • Stress and nervous system dysregulation – Constant stress keeps your body in survival mode, where digestion slows down.
  • Imbalanced gut flora – Low microbial diversity or bacterial overgrowth makes even simple foods harder to tolerate.

While there’s no one-size-fits-all answer, there are holistic strategies that can help calm bloating and support digestion naturally. Think of these as gentle, supportive practices you can start layering into your routine:

  • Slow down and practice mindful eating – Chew thoroughly, avoid distractions, and allow your body time to digest.
  • Try herbal support – Teas like ginger, peppermint, fennel, or chamomile relax the digestive tract and reduce gas buildup.
  • Belly breathing before meals – A few deep breaths before eating help shift you into “rest and digest” mode.
  • Gentle movement after eating – Walking, yoga twists, or stretching support food movement through the gut.
  • Hydrate wisely – Sip water throughout the day instead of drinking large amounts during meals.
  • Support your nervous system – Prayer, journaling, breathwork, or time outdoors regulate stress and improve digestion.

Food & Nutrition Suggestions

When it comes to food, bloating isn’t always about what you eat, but also how and when you eat. Small adjustments can make a big difference.

  • Start a food + symptom journal – Track meals and symptoms to uncover patterns and triggers.
  • Try a gentle elimination approach – Remove common triggers (gluten, dairy, soy, corn, processed foods) for a few weeks, then slowly reintroduce.
  • Choose easy-to-digest meals – Soups, stews, and cooked veggies are easier on the gut than raw salads or cold foods.
  • Add fiber gradually – Build up slowly to avoid overwhelming your gut and worsening bloating.
  • Season meals with digestion-friendly spices – Cumin, coriander, fennel, and ginger all aid digestion.
  • Don’t fear balanced meals – Protein, healthy fats, and fiber-rich carbs together stabilize blood sugar and ease digestive stress.

When to Dig Deeper

If you feel like you’ve “tried everything” and still experience bloating daily, your body may be asking for a deeper look.

Some red flags that it’s time to investigate further:

  • Bloating happens every single day, no matter what you eat.
  • You also struggle with fatigue, acne, brain fog, or irregular periods.
  • Your stomach looks like it’s “six months pregnant” by the end of the day.
  • Over-the-counter fixes only provide temporary relief.

Possible deeper root causes include:

  • Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth
  • Gut dysbiosis or candida overgrowth
  • Low stomach acid or digestive enzyme deficiency
  • Food sensitivities or intolerances
