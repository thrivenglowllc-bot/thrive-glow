Menopause: What’s Happening in Your Body & How to Ease Symptoms

October 2, 2025 · {{owner_name}}

If you’re a woman over 30, chances are you’ve heard the word menopause tossed around — sometimes with dread, sometimes with relief. But many women don’t fully understand what’s happening in their bodies during this natural transition or how they can support themselves holistically through it.

As an integrative health practitioner specializing in hormones, gut health, and women’s wellness, I want to break it down in a way that feels both empowering and science-based.

What Is Menopause?

Menopause marks the end of a woman’s reproductive years and is officially diagnosed after 12 consecutive months without a menstrual period. While the average age of menopause in the U.S. is around 51, the process begins years earlier with perimenopause, usually in the late 30s to early 40s.

During perimenopause, levels of estrogen and progesterone — two key reproductive hormones — begin to fluctuate unpredictably. This hormonal rollercoaster leads to many of the symptoms women often experience:

  • Hot flashes and night sweats
  • Irregular or heavy periods
  • Mood swings and irritability
  • Trouble sleeping
  • Brain fog and memory changes
  • Weight gain (especially around the midsection)
  • Digestive changes and bloating

Eventually, estrogen and progesterone reach consistently lower levels, and the body adapts to this “new normal.” But the transition isn’t always smooth. Supporting your body with holistic strategies can make a huge difference.

3 Things You Can Do Now to Support Your Body Through Menopause

1. Balance Blood Sugar to Calm Hormonal Swings

Hormones don’t work in isolation — they’re deeply connected to insulin (your blood sugar hormone). When blood sugar is unstable from too much caffeine, sugar, or processed carbs, symptoms like hot flashes, mood swings, and fatigue often worsen.

What you can do today:

  • Eat protein, healthy fat, and fiber with every meal to stabilize energy.
  • Swap out refined carbs (like white bread) for whole food carbs (like sweet potatoes or quinoa).
  • Don’t skip meals — going long stretches without food can trigger cortisol spikes and worsen symptoms.

This is a quick win because you’ll likely notice more stable energy and fewer sugar cravings within days.

2. Support Gut Health to Improve Hormone Detoxification

Your gut and liver play a huge role in processing and eliminating excess estrogen. If digestion is sluggish, estrogen can recirculate, leading to symptoms like bloating, mood swings, and heavy periods.

What you can do today:

  • Increase fiber intake from vegetables, flax, and chia seeds to support elimination.
  • Drink plenty of water to help flush toxins.
  • Consider adding fermented foods (like sauerkraut or kefir) to balance your gut microbiome.

Long-term strategy: Functional stool or hormone testing can give personalized insights into how well your body is metabolizing estrogen and which support tools (like probiotics, digestive enzymes, or specific herbs) are best for you.

3. Prioritize Sleep to Reduce Stress and Hot Flashes

Poor sleep is one of the most frustrating symptoms of perimenopause and menopause. Estrogen helps regulate your sleep cycle, and when it drops, women often struggle with insomnia, restless sleep, or night sweats.

What you can do today:

  • Keep your bedroom cool and dark — a drop in core body temperature helps trigger sleep.
  • Avoid caffeine after 12 p.m. and alcohol in the evening (both can worsen night sweats).
  • Try a calming bedtime ritual: gentle stretching, magnesium-rich tea, or journaling to lower cortisol.

Long-term strategy: Certain herbs (like ashwagandha or valerian root) and lifestyle practices (like mindfulness or acupuncture) can help regulate stress hormones and improve sleep quality.

Menopause is not the end of vitality — it’s simply a new chapter in your health journey. With the right nutrition, gut support, and lifestyle strategies, you can reduce uncomfortable symptoms and actually feel more balanced and vibrant.
